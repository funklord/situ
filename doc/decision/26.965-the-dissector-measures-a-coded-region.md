# 26.965: the dissector measures a coded region

Status: recorded
Date: 2026-11-12
Source: the dissector run against the walker over the coded-region sweep

**A `coded` or `sealed` region the data sizes was shown at its minimum,
and everything after it was misplaced.** `traverse.byte_span` answers a
placement's span with `size_bits`, and for such a region that is the
fixed part of its interior:

    struct s { u8 n; coded body(c) { u16 x; u8 data[n]; } u8 k; }

showed `body` as two bytes and `k` at offset 3 for any `n`, with every
codec -- the length-preserving ones too. And a region behind a member the
data sizes, where `byte_span` is None, reached `_length`, which had no
rule for a region and so declined or measured wrong: a `ratio_exact(5,
4)` region after `pre[m]` put `k` on the wrong byte.

The four backends and both walkers measure a region by one rule,
`traverse.region_extent`: the interior's fixed part plus each member the
data sizes, through the codec's expansion. The dissector does now, in the
integer arithmetic C's `_region_length` uses -- a ratio floored, a padded
ratio in whole groups -- and declines where that rule has no closed form
or the interior is versioned.

## How it was found

The coded-region sweep (26.962) compared six readers; the dissector was
then run against the walker over the same 56 schemas and differed on six
-- every `outer_n_first` shape and `r54_scalars_after`. After the change,
976 comparisons over them agree.

`test_the_dissector_measures_a_coded_region.py` pins the region's span
and `k`'s offset by hand for four expansions and three `n`, and fails
with the change reverted.
