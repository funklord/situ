# 26.1043: the walkers place a located struct from the message

Status: recorded
Date: 2026-11-17
Source: asking the two walkers about 26.1041's chain, which the four
backends had just been made to agree on

**The walkers disagreed with the backends, and with each other, on FS2's
chain.** On `chain following at head.next` under `[depth = 8]`, a section
pointing at itself was OK to the Python walker, "cannot say" to the C
walker, and CONSTRAINT to the four backends. A chain of twelve was BOUNDS to
both walkers where the backends refuse it for its depth.

Three faults, two of them in the Python walker:

- **A located nested struct was bounded by its parent's frame.** Every link
  of a chain is a nested view the size of one section, and the next section
  lies outside it, so every link that did not point back into its own
  section was BOUNDS. 9.8 and 26.446 say a located member reaches past the
  frame by construction; `walk.frame_end` now answers the message's end for
  one, and the nested-member check, `_sub_view`, the nested size and the
  `[holds]` depth probe ask it.
- **A nested "cannot say" read as a pass.** `_validate_here` returned a
  nested verdict with `if verdict:`, so `None` -- past the ceiling, or a
  struct this image cannot judge -- fell through as OK. The self-loop
  reached the ceiling and was accepted. `None` is returned now, as the arm
  path's caller already did (26.790).
- **The C walker measured a located offset from the nested struct.**
  `validate_deep` recurses with `message + at / 8` as the new base, so an
  `at` inside a nested struct was taken from the wrong byte at every link
  after the first. The image binding now carries the root message and its
  length, set by `situ_walk_validate` and `situ_walk_failed_check`, and a
  located nested struct is validated over its own extent from the root.

## Measured

`test_a_located_chain.py::test_both_walkers`: the five chains give one
answer in both walkers -- BOUNDS where a chain ends outside the message,
"cannot say" past the depth, which is the walkers' answer for every
recursion (26.286) where the backends say CONSTRAINT.

## Still open

**A struct nested inside a recursion counts against the ceiling.** The
walkers count every nested struct in one descent, and a struct with no
`[depth]` of its own gets `WALK_DEPTH_MAX`, 8. So each section's `hdr`, nine
levels down a chain the schema allows eight deep, is past its ceiling, and a
legal nine-section chain is "cannot say" in both. That predates this record,
answers no wrong verdict, and caps any chain like FS2's (`[depth = 64]`) at
eight in both walkers.
