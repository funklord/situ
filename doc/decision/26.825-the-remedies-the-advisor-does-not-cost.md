# 26.825: the remedies the advisor does not cost

Status: recorded -- a measurement; whether 18.2 gains further rows is the
holder's, as 26.822 established and 26.824 confirmed. The headline count
below was first written as "costs 11 of them", which conflated the
advisor's ELEVEN RULES with the ROWS they cost; re-counted by mapping each
rule to its row, it is ten, and two rules answer no row at all.
Date: 2026-11-03
Source: `situc/propagate.py`'s `TABLE`; `situc/advise.py`'s `CATALOG`;
`project.md` sections 1 and 18.2

Section 18 calls the advisor the differentiator and says the reason is the
cost column: *"move your variable-length fields to the end" is folklore
anyone can repeat, and "moving `opts` after `recs` costs 0 bytes and returns
4 members to absolute addressing" is advice.*

**The propagation table states 57 remedies and the advisor's eleven rules
cost ten of them.** So the remaining distance on section 1's third aim is
not a count of causes without advice -- 26.822 counted those, and counted
them off diagnostic clauses -- it is the set of remedies situ already prints
to a reader with no price beside them. **47 of the 57 are that set.**

Ten rather than eleven because the two bit-field rows are one rule (26.824
says why) and because **two rules answer no propagation row at all**:
`fill-alignment-holes` fires off a weakening the lattice recorded rather
than off a row of its own -- 18.2 says so, and the row that noticed is the
one that knows -- and `group-covered-regions` is a structural observation
about where regions sit, which no single member's weakening states. Both
are advice without a blame clause behind them, which is the opposite of the
gap this record measures and worth knowing for the same reason.

## How this is counted, and why not the way 26.822 counted

26.822 counted `caused by:` clauses. A clause is printed where the axis a
rule asked about is the one that was weakened, so a clause count counts
*questions asked*, not constructs present -- which is how it reported 4, 3
and 2 for three populations that are 29, 2 and 50 (26.824).

`Resolved.weakenings` is the honest instrument: every row that fired on
every member, whether or not anybody asked. Counted that way over the 45
schemas the corpus parses standalone, and crossed against the subjects
`advise.suggest` returns, so a member another rule already advises is not
counted as bare.

## The candidates, by members getting no advice at all

Every one of the 47 was enumerated, not just the head: the last row below
has the smallest population and is the only one that costs nothing, which
is exactly the shape a list read top-down loses.

| bare members | propagation row | the remedy it prints |
|---|---|---|
| 121 | `unaligned-multi-byte-scalar` | insert `reserved` padding, or reorder, to land the field on its natural boundary |
| 42 | `delimited-member` | `until D max N` bounds the scan |
| 33 | `dynamic-element-type` | give the element a fixed size, or use an `indexed` region |
| 31 | `scanned-predecessor` | put the fixed-offset members before the first delimited one -- free |
| 19 | `register-partial-word` | a field occupying the whole `access_width` needs no masking |
| 7 | `non-minimal-varint` + `non-minimal-text-number` | declare `[minimal]`, which refuses the second spelling of a value |

**The first is the one worth arguing about.** 18.2's existing alignment row
reads *"unaligned members with padding present | zero cost"*, and that
trigger is why `fill-alignment-holes` fires **4 times** across the corpus
while 121 statically-placed multi-byte scalars sit off their boundary with
the remedy printed and no cost. The row is not wrong -- where padding is
already present the fix is free, and printing that is the point -- but
"insert padding" is a different row with a different cost, not a widening of
this one.

**The last row is the only FREE one and was nearly missed**, which is why
the enumeration was finished rather than stopped at the head of the list.
`[minimal]` costs zero bytes -- it narrows what the parser accepts and
changes nothing on the wire -- and buys the `canonical` axis, which no
other row here can be had for nothing. Seven instances across the corpus,
and the reason it does not look like a candidate is that it is small in
every dimension except price.

It carries the one caveat 18.3 already draws: `[minimal]` refuses inputs
the schema accepted before, so it is free as a COST and not free as a
compatibility change. That is `situc wire`'s question rather than the
advisor's, and 18.3 says in as many words that one tool cannot serve both
orderings.

`bmp`'s header is the case to read: `file_size` is a `u32` at byte 2 and
`pixel_offset` a `u32` at byte 10, both inherited from a format that
predates alignment mattering. Two bytes of `reserved` ahead of each turns a
masked multi-byte load into one instruction, and nothing in situ says so with
a number.

The 170 unaligned scalars split 121 static and 49 dynamic, and only the
static half is a gap: where the offset itself is dynamic the remedy is to
move the field ahead of the variable-length member, which
`move-dynamic-to-tail` already costs from the other side.

## What is NOT a candidate, and the distinction is the useful half

**A remedy that tells the reader how to use the generated code is not an
advisor row.** The advisor's product is a schema change with a price; a
sentence about which accessor to call has no schema change in it and no
bytes to count. Three rows are that shape and between them they are the
largest populations in the table:

- `non-native-endian-scalar`, 466 bare -- *"no pointer accessor is generated
  for this field; use the by-value getter"*.
- `frame-relative`, 239 bare -- *"acquire a view of the frame once"*.
- `text-number`, 38 bare -- *"read it through the generated getter"*.

Counting remedies without reading them would have made these three the
headline and they are not findings at all. Nor are `skipped-lead`,
`endian-native` or `bcd`: each is a fact about a format situ is describing
rather than designing, which is section 2's territory and 26.822's own
dividing line.

## The option, its cost, and whose it is

- **The option:** up to six further rows in 18.2 -- the alignment one first
  by population, the `[minimal]` one first by price.
- **The cost:** per row, a `find` function in the shape of the eleven
  existing ones, a row in the spec table, a test that watches it fire, and a
  test below the taken-advice marker that rewrites the schema and measures
  what the advice bought -- which is what caught the costing error in
  26.824's bit-field row. The alignment row additionally needs a padding
  computation the existing row does not have, since it must choose where the
  padding goes rather than find it.
- **Whose:** yours. 26.822 settled that a row in 18.2 is a change to what the
  product is rather than a defect to fix, and the answer that produced 26.824
  was for three named rows.

**And the measurement keeps whatever is decided.** "Eleven rules against 57
printed remedies" was not knowable from either file, and the three
usage-advice rows at the top of the population are the reason the count alone
would have been misleading in the opposite direction from 26.822's.
