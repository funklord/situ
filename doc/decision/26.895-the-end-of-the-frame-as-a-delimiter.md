# 26.895: The end of the frame as a delimiter

Status: recorded
Date: 2026-11-06
Source: hull, and the holder's rule that a sane protocol's need is ours

    u8 line[] until "\n" | end;

A text format's last line, or a comment at the end of a file, stops at the
delimiter or at the frame's end, and `until` calls the second a truncated
member. That is right for a protocol and wrong for these. `end` after a `|` is
a soft word, read only where a delimiter could stand, and it says the frame's
end may stand for the delimiter.

**It is one decision.** The scanners already stop at the frame and report
whether the delimiter was there, and an extent already adds it only if it was.
Only `validate` refused, and all five readers of that question ask
`traverse.must_be_terminated`. So the construct is a parser word, a flag, and a
line in that predicate, and no backend or walker changed.

The last run judged this about 250 sites and below the bar on value, counting
every place a delimiter is touched. That was the wrong count: the sites that
needed changing were one. A size estimated by grep is the occurrences of a word
and not the work.

The wire signature writes `until:0a|end`, since a sender relying on it and a
receiver refusing it do not interoperate. A builder still writes the delimiter.
