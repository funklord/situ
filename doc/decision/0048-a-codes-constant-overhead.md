# 0048: a stuffing code's overhead is a ratio and a constant

Status: accepted 2026-09-04; not yet built
Date: 2026-09-04
Phase: raised by 26.147, settled by the copyright holder with the other open
items

## Context

`ratio_bounded(a, b)` is what a stuffing codec publishes about how much its
output can exceed its input, and a consumer sizes a buffer from it. 26.147
measured every generated encoder against its own declaration and found three
that write one byte more than they promise:

    cobs        254 in -> 256 out      declared 255 for 254
    hdlc         40 in ->  48 out      declared   6 for 5
    ppp_async    16 in ->  33 out      declared   2 for 1
    slip         16 in ->  33 out      declared   2 for 1
    smtp_dot     48 in ->  64 out      declared   4 for 3

HDLC and SMTP follow their ratios exactly. COBS, SLIP and PPP each write the
same extra byte: the frame delimiter the encoder appends. Confirmed constant
at four lengths for COBS and three for the others -- 1 in gives 3 out, 508
gives 511 -- so it is an addend and not a rounding artefact.

**A consumer sizing from the published bound overruns by one on every
encode**, and nothing in the suite could catch it from the declaration side:
every committed schema declares the ratio the table declares, so the
declarations and the generators agree with each other and both disagree with
what the encoder writes. It took measuring the object file.

## The fork

Either the delimiter is the codec's output and the signature is short by one,
or the delimiter is framing and three encoders should stop writing it.

## Decision

**The delimiter is the code's, and `ratio_bounded` gains a constant term.**

The signature becomes a ratio *and* an addend -- `capmap` renders one or the
other today and never both, which is the limitation rather than a position.
A code that expands by `a/b` and then appends `k` bytes says so, and a
consumer allocates `len * a / b + k`.

Two things decide it, and neither is a preference.

**The encoder must emit what its decoder consumes.** A COBS or SLIP decoder
finds the end of a frame by the delimiter. An encoder that omitted it would
produce output its own decoder cannot bound, so `decode(encode(x))` would not
close within one buffer -- and a codec whose round trip needs a byte supplied
from outside is not one situ can hold to a signature at all.

**The generated code already says the number, in prose, in the place a caller
reads.** The emitted C carries "`out` needs 2 * len + 1 bytes: twice the
payload if every byte has to be escaped, plus the trailing delimiter". The
emitter knows the constant and the header tells the truth; only the
machine-readable signature omits it. That is not a disagreement about what a
code does, it is a gap in what the signature can express -- which is the
cheaper of the two repairs and the one that leaves the byte stream alone.

And 26.147's own title is the position: *a named code's overhead is the
code's*.

## Alternatives considered

**The delimiter is framing, and the three encoders drop it.** Defensible on
0032's layering, which puts framing on a rung above the codec, and it makes
the existing signature accurate with no grammar change. Rejected on the round
trip above: it changes what three encoders emit, breaks every consumer of the
current output, and leaves a decoder that consumes a byte its encoder does
not write. Correct layering is not worth an asymmetric codec.

**Fold the constant into the ratio.** `ratio_bounded(256, 254)` covers COBS
at 254 input bytes and under-covers at 1, where the true output is 3 and the
ratio gives 2. A constant overhead is not a ratio at any length, which is the
measurement's own point.

**Leave the signature and document the discrepancy.** This is what the tree
did by accident for as long as the codes have existed, and 26.147 is the
record of what it cost: a published bound that is wrong by one, in a number
whose only purpose is to size a buffer.

## Consequences

- The signature grammar gains the addend, and `capmap` renders both terms.
  A code declaring only a ratio is unchanged and means addend zero.
- `kernels.STUFFING_BOUNDS` gains the constant per code, and
  `test_every_stuffing_code_expands_by_the_amount_it_is_measured_at`
  compares against ratio-plus-addend rather than ratio alone.
- The wire signature carries it, for 0043's reason: a peer sizing to a
  different bound disagrees about what fits.
- Three of the five codes gain `+1`; HDLC and SMTP gain `+0` and are
  unaffected. No generated byte stream changes.
