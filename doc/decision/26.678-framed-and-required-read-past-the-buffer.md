# 26.678: `framed` and `required` read past the buffer

Status: recorded
Date: 2026-10-23
Source: `project.md` section 26

**26.675's sweep took the validate and extent chain and left framed and
required.** bbq-predictor said so ten hours before this was written, in a
message that sat unread in situ's inbox while the sweep was being pushed.
Reproduced here at `b177e5d`, which is the pushed tip:

    ERROR: AddressSanitizer: heap-buffer-overflow
    #0 situ_varint_get runtime/c/situ.h:1744
    #1 situ::one::v_len() const
    #2 situ::one::framed(unsigned int&) const
    #3 situ::one::required(...)
    #4 situ::s::framed(unsigned int&) const
    #5 situ::s::required(...)

The schema is a counted run of varint-bearing records after a run the same
count sizes:

    struct one { uvar v; }
    struct s { u8 n; u8 pad[n]; one run[n]; }

With `n = 8` in a two-byte message, `s::framed` hands `one::required` a
length of `have - at` with `at` already past the buffer, and the varint
reader walks from a pointer outside it.

## What this corrects

**It was item 1(c) of their first report, recorded as "not re-run here"
and then not re-run here either.** 26.675's appendix listed it among
items carried as reported; the sweep that record describes went through
the validate and extent chain and the subtraction sites, and framed and
required were not in it.

So the sentence in 26.676 -- "the memory-safety hole of 26.675 is closed
and the verdict is not" -- is true of the validate path and false as
written. `suggestion/bbq-predictor.md` said item 1 was FIXED without that
qualification, to a consumer deciding whether to ship a client parser.
Both are corrected, and this record is what they point at.

**The one mitigating fact is theirs rather than situ's**: they do not call
`framed` or `required`, parsing whole size-capped files instead, and said
so in the same message. So the hole situ left open is not one they are
exposed to. That is luck about their design, not a property of the fix.

## Why it was missed, which is the part worth keeping

`build-and-commit.md` gives three triggers for reading the inbox: on
arriving in a tree, **whenever you fetch**, and before finishing. The
`SessionStart` hook covers only the first, and the fetch trigger exists
precisely for a session already resident -- which this one was, for the
whole of a day.

This session fetched about a dozen times and never re-checked, because
the inbox had been read once at the start and that was treated as the
check having been done. **An inbox read once is a snapshot, exactly as a
guidelines file read once is**, and the same document says so about both.
The message that mattered arrived afterwards, nine hours into the work it
was about, and reported that the fix being built was incomplete.

The cost was not the ten hours of delay. It was that the sweep was
recorded as complete, pushed, and reported to its reporter as FIXED,
while the correction sat two directories away.
