# 26.667: What `at <literal>` is, and the offset axis has no value for it

Status: recorded
Date: 2026-10-21
Source: `project.md` section 26, from `suggestion/ossacli.md`

`ossacli` described a BMIC structure the way its datasheet does -- a list
of absolute offsets -- and reported that the spelling costs a
capability:

    u32   signature            at 1;
    u32   board_id             at 26;
    u8    cache_battery_count  at 142;

Every field comes out `offset=DataPlaced`; padding the same layout out
with explicit `u8 gap[n]` members gives `AbsoluteStatic(...)`. They
offered two readings -- "a gap, or `at` being for data-dependent
placement only" -- and asked for a word in the docs either way.

**Reproduced, and it is four axes rather than one.** Measured over the
two structs side by side:

    struct located  size=0    address=Unstable
      signature     offset=DataPlaced  align=Unaligned  address=Unstable
    struct padded   size=143
      signature     offset=AbsoluteStatic(0x01)  align=Aligned(1)

So the located form loses the offset, the struct's extent, every
alignment and the address stability. `size=0` is the sharpest of those:
a consumer sizing a buffer from the map allocates nothing for a
structure whose last field is at 142.

## The answer is neither of the two offered

**It was built as the first reading and reverted.** Setting `offset_bits`
from the folded literal, and asking `_is_data_placed` for the RESULT
rather than the spelling, produces exactly what `ossacli` expected:
`AbsoluteStatic(0x01)`, `AbsoluteStatic(0x1A)`, `AbsoluteStatic(0x8E)`,
with the alignments the padded form derives and no `Unstable`. It also
reports a number from the wrong frame.

**`at` is measured from the start of the MESSAGE** (9.8, in those
words), and `offset_bits` is struct-relative everywhere else -- the
generated accessor for a located member takes a `situ_msg_t *` and
bounds-checks against `msg->size` precisely because the view cannot
answer. The two bases coincide only for a struct acquired at message
offset zero, which is why the reproduction above looks right. So the
first reading is a map that lies whenever a struct is read at an offset.

**And the second reading is not right either.** `DataPlaced`'s stated
meaning is "the number comes from the message rather than from the
members before it, so nothing about the frame bounds it and it may
point anywhere". For `at 26` the first half is false -- the number is a
compile-time constant -- and the second half is true. `address =
Unstable` is justified as "a pointer to it moves whenever the field
holding its offset is written", and for a literal there is no such
field.

So a statically-but-message-relatively placed member is a third thing,
and **the offset axis has no value for it**: `AbsoluteStatic(n)` and
`FrameStatic(n)` are both offsets a reader resolves from the frame it
holds, and this one is resolved from the message. The axis is 2049's
lattice and adding a value to it is the copyright holder's.

## What is decided, and what the holder decides

Decided, and no change: the spelling does not lose anything a reader
can act on wrongly. Every generated accessor bounds-checks on each call
against the message, which is what `DataPlaced` is telling a reader it
costs, and that remains true of a literal.

The holder's, in one question: **should the offset axis distinguish a
message-relative constant?** A `MessageStatic(n)` would let the map say
the offset is known, keep the per-call bounds check that message
relativity requires, and drop the `Unstable` that a literal does not
earn. The cost is a sixth value on the axis every backend, the walker
and the dissector render, for a construct three formats use -- and
`capability.py` holds the axis to its documented order, so the value
has a place in the lattice to be argued about rather than chosen.

Not raised as a gap, because what `ossacli` asked for is in the docs
now: `at` is message-relative and that is why its capability reads the
way it does.
