# 26.934: a dissector extent reads a field it has walked past

Status: recorded
Date: 2026-11-08
Source: a DNS reply in real tshark, beside Wireshark's own DNS dissector

**An extent function captures a field that a later length reads, where it stands**, at
the running `n`, as the dissector's body captures one at `at`. A field placed after a
variable member has no constant offset, and only the body could read one: DNS's
`resource` hands every `rdata` arm `size = rdlength`, and `rdlength` stands after the
owner name. So `resource` had no extent, the count of answers was declined, and a reply
showed its question and none of its answers. What a member hands its child is now among
what is captured, as sizes and discriminants are.

**A variant's arms are measured where the variant starts.** Inside an extent function
every member is measured from where it starts and the arms were measured from the
struct's start. DNS's arms are sized by argument, so nothing there showed it; a record
whose arm is measured from its own bytes, after a variable member, consumed 9 bytes of
15 that way.

**An arm that takes arguments is dissected, inside `handing`**, as a nested member that
takes them is, rather than stepped over.

Held to Wireshark's DNS dissector on the `every_arm` reply: five resources' types, TTLs,
lengths, the MX preference and the address agree. The arm's offset is held by a
hand-worked record, which fails with the arms measured from the struct's start.
