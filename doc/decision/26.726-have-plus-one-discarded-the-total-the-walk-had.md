# 26.726: `have + 1` discarded the total the walk already had

Status: recorded -- fixed in all four backends
Date: 2026-10-29
Source: `project.md` section 26

Found by 26.725's sweep the moment it was pointed at every schema this
repository builds rather than at `example/` only. **Three structs in
`test/schema/edges.situ` break monotonicity**, and the fault is older than
either of the two faults that week -- it is present unchanged at `97d1343`,
`a176c43` and `f867edc`.

    framed_             fill=0x00  have=4  need FELL to 5 from 6
    stuffed_seal        fill=0x00  have=1  need FELL to 2 from 17
    stuffed_then_field  fill=0x00  have=1  need FELL to 2 from 3

## One line, in two branches, in four backends

Two branches of `required` answer `have + 1`: a varint whose bytes have not
all arrived, and a delimited member whose terminator is not in what we have.
Both are right about themselves -- how much more a continuation byte or an
unseen delimiter promises is a question only the sender can answer, so one
more byte is the honest bound on *that member*.

**It is not a bound on the message**, and that is the whole of this. By the
time either branch runs, `at` already holds every fixed byte of the struct
plus every variable member the walk has measured. `framed` is the clearest:

    struct framed {
        u8   magic[4];
        u8   version[] until "\r\n" max 16;
        u8   label[]   until "\0";
        u16  count;
    }

`at` opens at **6** -- four bytes of `magic` and two of `count`. For three
bytes the guard fires and reports that 6. At four bytes the guard passes,
`version` is not terminated, and the branch answers `have + 1` = **5**. So
the answer falls, and a caller that waited for six bytes is told to wait for
five.

This is the third instance in one week of the same shape, and the shape is
worth more than any of them: **two quantities, computed in different places
from different things, where neither bounds the other.** 26.693 was a
constraint-derived floor against a data-derived total. 26.724 was a
constraint-derived per-element minimum against a data-derived element bound.
This is a per-member bound against the struct's own running total. Each time
the fix is to make the two answer to one authority.

## The fix

    *need = have + 1u;
    if (*need < at) {
            *need = at;
    }

and the expression form where the backend has one -- Rust
`(have + 1).max(at)`, Python `max(have + 1, at)`. Both terms are lower
bounds on the same total, so their maximum is one too, and it cannot fall
below an answer already given because `at` never decreases.

`framed` answers `6 6 6 6 6 6 7 8 9 10 11` for the first eleven prefixes
now, where it answered `6 6 6 6 5 6 7 8 9 10 11`. Measured in all four
backends, identical, with Rust compiled under `-D warnings`.

**What it does not do is tighten the bound**, and it should not pretend to.
Six is still well below the nine `framed` really needs -- two bytes of
`version`'s terminator, one of `label`'s, and the two of `count`. Reading
each delimited member's own minimum into the total is the same open question
26.693 parked with the copyright holder: whether `required` should read
every length that is readable so that the first answer is the last. This
makes the answer monotone without taking a position on that.

## Why nothing saw it

Three things had to be true at once, and they were, for as long as this
function has existed:

- **Nothing ran `required` over `edges.situ`.** 26.725 built the corpus
  sweep against `example/` only, which is the very mistake
  `test/unit/every_schema.py` was written to stop -- its docstring says six
  files wanted the list of schemas and four had the shorter one, and names
  `edges.situ` as the file most likely to break a backend because it exists
  to carry what the examples do not. The sweep made that mistake again and
  it was corrected before being committed twice.
- **The shape needs fixed bytes on BOTH sides of a delimited member.**
  `at` must exceed the prefix the guard reports, so there has to be
  something fixed *after* the variable member too. Nothing in
  `example/` has that arrangement with a delimiter, and none of the eight
  shapes in `test_required_answers_every_prefix.py` does either -- `lone`
  puts its varint at offset 0, so `at` is 1 and `have + 1` is 1 at zero
  bytes, equal rather than below.
- **The probe returned on its first finding.** `framed_` is positionally
  first, so `stuffed_seal` and `stuffed_then_field` were invisible until
  the probe was changed to count findings and carry on. **That change was
  made before this fix landed and it immediately tripled the finding**,
  which is the sweep-is-the-defect lens pointed at a sweep written the day
  before: a gate that stops at the first fault reports one fault per run,
  and the run that finds the second is the one after the first is fixed.

The corpus construct that 26.724's record asked for is 26.727, landing
next and separately: it closes a different blind spot and would have found
none of these three.
