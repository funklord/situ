# 26.953: a fixed-width `[minimal]` number compiled nowhere in C++

Status: recorded
Date: 2026-11-10
Source: a sweep of every text-number shape through every generator and every reader

**`decimal u32 n[4] [minimal]` at a constant offset gave a C++ header that
did not compile.** The spelling check started the digits at
`n_offset()`, and a member at a constant offset has no such accessor; it
starts at the offset expression, as `sum`'s run does. C, Rust, Python and
both walkers read the shape and agree.

No corpus schema has it -- cpio's fixed numbers carry no `[minimal]` --
which is why the lens came from 26.951 rather than from a test: a
construct exercised in one of its forms is unexercised in the others. So
every shape was built: three radices, three forms (fixed, delimited,
sized by a field) and four kinds (unsigned, signed, `[minimal]`,
`[padded]`), through the four backends, the dissector, the checks, `doc`
and the packer, and then through all six readers over valid and near-miss
inputs. Every accepted shape built everywhere, the refusals were the
intended ones (a signed fixed width; `[padded]` off a written width), and
this was the one disagreement.

`test_a_fixed_width_number_may_be_minimal.py` holds it in all four and
both walkers, and fails with the fix removed.
