# 26.1081: a registry is generated in C -- a table, find, check, value, safer

Status: recorded
Date: 2026-11-18
Source: 0070, step 3

**What fuzznet hand-writes as `fzn_schema_entry_t` and its parsers,
generated.** Per registry, C emits a table of entries and four functions
over it:

- `_find(key, len)`: the entry a key is -- exact, or a family's instance,
  the family's key then one word of `a-z0-9`, as `fzn_schema_find`'s;
- `_check(entry, bytes, len)`: the bytes are a cell of the entry -- its
  `min_size`/`max_size`, then its type's own view and `validate`, then
  its `min`/`max` on the value;
- `_value(entry, bytes, len, &out)`: what the cell is worth, through the
  type's getter -- its `let value`, or its one integer or text-number
  member;
- `_safer(entry, a, b)`: whether `b` is strictly safer than `a` by the
  entry's `safe` direction, 0 where either is not a cell of it.

A row carries the key, family, safe, bounds, default, unit, summary and
window, and one bitmask per declared policy, each word a bit
(`SITU_SCOPE_ESTATE`). The parse is the type's, so what `_check` refuses is
exactly what a reader of the cell refuses: "the parsers are the readers'
too", fuzznet's header's own requirement.

## The rule

- A cell is the whole of its bytes, so its type is fixed-size -- the
  length must be its size -- or runs to the end of its frame through a
  `[remaining]` member. A type ending anywhere else is refused when the C is
  generated, rather than leaving bytes nothing checks.
- A sole token member is not a value; a type says what a token is worth
  with a `let value`, `(word == flag_word.on) | (word == flag_word.one)`.
