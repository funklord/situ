# 26.656: The move, and three checks that did not cover what I cited

Status: recorded
Date: 2026-10-19
Source: written as a record; section 26 has been records since this entry

Section 26's 655 entries are one file each under `doc/decision/`, and
`project.md` is 8,367 lines and about 104,000 tokens -- half a context
window, from 49,051 lines and three windows when 26.608 measured it.
26.655 carries the passes, the rate and the projection that sent the
work this way instead of the cut.

**The naming and the append-only exemption are in `project.md` rule 5**,
both set by the holder: a finding is `26.NNN-slug.md` and keeps the
number it is cited by, where a decision is `NNNN-slug.md`; and the
append-only rule is the decision's, findings being a living log.
`test_a_record_says_which_rule_governs_it` holds the `Status:` line to
that partition, 655 findings against 62 decisions.

## What the tooling got wrong, which is the transferable part

Three checks in a row reported success over something they were not
looking at. Not one was a bug in the code under test.

**`split.py` claimed the style gate proved its pointers resolve.**
`doc_paths` reads TABLE ROWS only, deliberately -- a path in prose is as
often an example or a glob -- so the gate's "names no missing file" never
saw a prose pointer. It reported `4 path(s)` over a 47,000-line document
and I read that as covering ten pointers. `--check` is the check that
sentence described.

**`cut.py`'s heading invariant was over-strict and refused a correct
write.** It required every heading to survive, conflating a numbered
entry heading -- cited from elsewhere, never to move -- with a prose
`####` subheading, which nothing can cite and which belongs to the
subsection it titles. Since narration subsections are most of what a cut
removes, it would have blocked every pass. Relaxed to numbered headings,
with all three controls re-run rather than assumed.

**And the retention control ran without the config it was testing.** A
passing and a failing test both kept their trees, which looks exactly
like the setting not working; the run reported `rootdir: .../scratchpad`
and no configfile, pytest taking its rootdir from the arguments. With
`-c pyproject.toml` only the failure's tree survived. The two runs
together are the A/B (26.657).

## The fourth was a gap I diagnosed and the tree did not have

**This record exists because its own number was cited before it was
written** -- `(26.656)` from `tool/cut.py`, `tool/split.py` and
`test/unit/test_conventions.py`, four sites written while the move was in
flight. I read that as a hole: `split.py --check` reads pointers in
`project.md`, `cut.py --check` reads references within it, and neither
looks at a citation from source, where 2,652 of them live. So I wrote a
test for it.

**The tree already had one, and mine was the worse copy.**
`test_every_section_this_tree_cites_exists` scans source for
PARENTHESISED citations -- which is the convention, and the reason a
bare `26.419` in a prose comment is not one -- and it carries a
deliberate ONE-NUMBER window: the next section 26 entry may be cited
before it exists, because 25.0 lands a finding as two commits and the
work half cites the entry the fold half adds. `26.656` was exactly that
number. The citations were inside the documented exception and the
window closed when this record was written.

My version matched any `26.NNN` anywhere and failed on its first run
against that prose `26.419` -- a number used as a value, in the comment
explaining the window. Removed rather than narrowed: *a second copy of
the condition is a second thing to be wrong*, and the second copy was
mine.

**The rule I did not run is `working-practice.md`'s**: before recording
something as a gap, look for whether the project already decided it
somewhere else under a different name. The name here was *sections this
tree cites* rather than *entries cited from source*, which is why
grepping for the second found nothing. That makes four for the session's
other shape too -- a check reporting success over something it was not
looking at -- except this one is the mirror: a check I believed absent
that was present and better.
