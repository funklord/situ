# 26.995: a terminator is looked for past the lead

Status: recorded
Date: 2026-11-13
Source: hull's inbox message of 20261113T1040Z, and the holder's yes to
26.606's first finding

**Set by the copyright holder 2026-11-13**, answering the question 26.606
left open: a run that ends at its terminator looks for the terminator past
the lead its element would skip. In `(a )` the space before `)` is the lead
of the element that would come next -- `form` opens with
`peek u8 kind skip` -- and that element turns out to be the terminator. So
`(a )` holds one item, and a list closed on its own line holds what it
appears to.

**What hull measured.** At b1ee9aba hull's `test/sexpr_test.c` failed 3 of
109 cases, the three it keeps by name for 26.606's finding: `(a )` and a
list closed on its own line counted 999 elements -- its own cap, the walk
running away -- and `(machine\n  (backend ns)\n)\n` was refused. Their
bisect over 6f807f01..b1ee9aba named 1fc9f26f, which validates a run's
elements: before it the space was a phantom element of extent 1 holding an
empty symbol, counted and accepted; after it every reader validated that
element and refused it, BOUNDS, because its symbol peeks past its view.
Reproduced here in all six readers before anything was changed. hull pinned
6f807f01 meanwhile.

**The rule, in every reader alike.** `traverse.element_lead` gives the
skip set of the element struct's first own member -- the only lead visible
from the run; a lead deeper inside the element is the element's business.
Where it is non-empty, each walk of the run measures the lead at the
cursor, asks for a terminator past it, and on a match steps the cursor over
the lead before the terminator is taken. Where no terminator stands there,
the cursor stays put and the lead is the element's own, as it always was.
A run whose element has no lead emits exactly what it did.

- C: two statements in `_record_prologue`'s loop, so the lead is measured
  once per element; every C walk of a record run goes through it.
- C++: an immediately invoked lambda in `_record_match`; Rust a block
  expression; Python a walrus. Each steps the cursor inside the
  condition, so every walk that asks moves alike.
- Both walkers: `walk.element_lead`, shared with `report._run_elements`,
  and the C walker's `element_lead` at its three record-run sites.
- The dissector: `situ_skip` before `situ_stands` in `_record_walk`. It
  showed no rows for the phantom (its content is zero bytes), so it was
  right before by accident; this keeps it in step with the readers.

`required` needed nothing: its walk asks each element for its own
requirement, and every case below answers the same before and after.

**Measured.** `test_a_terminator_is_looked_for_past_the_lead` takes hull's
reader shape and eight lists, hull's three among them: all four backends
agree on the count and validate 0, both walkers validate 0, and the
dissector spans the list and shows one `form.kind` per form. The first two
fail with the change reverted. The corpus regenerated old against new in
four targets and Lua: only `example/sexpr` changes, the one schema with
this shape.

**Not changed, and noticed.** An unterminated `(a b  ` counts three
items, the trailing whitespace being a form whose symbol runs to the end
of the frame; every reader agrees and `validate` refuses it as
unterminated. A message that ends before its terminator is not one hull
produces, so it is left as it stands.
