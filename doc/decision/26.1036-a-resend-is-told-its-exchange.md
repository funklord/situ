# 26.1036: a resend is told its exchange

Status: recorded
Date: 2026-11-14
Source: fuzznet, `suggestion/fuzznet.md` 2026-11-13 (gap 1) and 2026-11-14
(inbox 20261113T2359Z)

**fuzznet seals every datagram under a fresh nonce**, and the far end
refuses a replayed one, so a retransmission has to be resealed. Rung 6
handed `submit` the bytes alone and said nothing about which exchange they
were; with one exchange outstanding fuzznet could smuggle it through
`ctx`, which is the workaround the gap describes. 26.1009 left it until a
consumer was adopting; fuzznet now is, against both of its resend loops.

**Every driver takes an optional `resend`, told the exchange's id**, and
uses it for retransmissions only; the first send still goes to `submit`,
which the caller seals itself before `send`. Additive in all four:

- C: `<driver>_on_resend(drive, fn)`, the pointer on the driver and NULL
  from `init`. Not a field of `situ_io_t`: callers initialise that struct
  positionally, and a new field is `-Wmissing-field-initializers` under the
  `-Wextra -Werror` they build with.
- C++: a virtual `resend(id, data, len)` on `io`, defaulting to `submit`.
- Rust: a default method `resend(id, data)` on `Io`, defaulting to
  `submit`; `step` collects each due exchange's id with its bytes.
- Python: a keyword-only `resend` callable; without one, `submit` as
  before.

**Measured** in all four: exchange 7 sent at 0 with an 800 ms timeout,
stepped at 800, reaches the hook with 7 and `submit` once; and in Python
a driver with no hook still resends through `submit`.

**Not taken: the total deadline.** fuzznet resends every 600 ms and gives
up at 3 s whatever happened; a relation states one per-attempt timeout and
a retry count, so its total is always (retries + 1) x timeout. Stating a
resend interval and a total separately is a new relation attribute
(fuzznet proposes `[resend_ms = 600, retries = 3, timeout_ms = 3000]`),
which is schema surface and left to the holder.
