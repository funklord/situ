# 26.1071: a tag's covered span refuses a region the frame does not hold

Status: recorded
Date: 2026-11-18
Source: the walker's new `.covered` line (the measurement after 26.1069),
on its first comparison

**All four backends answered a covered span for a tag over a sealed
region that overran the frame.** `dtls.record` declares `fragment[length -
24]` inside `sealed(aes_128_gcm, ...)` and its tag after the region. A
42-byte record whose `length` reads 14081 got `tag.covered 21+21` from
every backend: the region clamped to the frame, the tag's offset placed
after the clamp, and the span from the region's start to the tag's offset
-- bytes the sender never covered, handed out as the span to verify.
`validate` called the record malformed. `example/packet` (`4+58`) and
`edges.blocked_arg` (`1+48`) the same.

26.1058 made `_covered` refuse a covered member whose declared length does
not fit the frame, through `_covered_fits` over `covered_members`. That
asks FIELDS, and a sealed or coded region's extent is its codec's, declared
by no field: the interior's `fragment` has a dotted path that
`_fits_check` declines, and the region placement is not a field. So the
one check that held such a region to the frame -- `_region_fits` (26.776),
in `validate` -- was never asked for the span.

**And a fixed covered member was never asked to fit either.**
`_covered_fits` skipped every member that declares no length, so in
`edges.covered_tail` -- `u8 pad[n]` then `authenticated body { beacon
head; }` -- a `pad` the frame clamped clamped `head` with it, and the span
came back `49+0`: nothing, at the frame's end, for a three-byte region.

## The rule

- `_covered_fits`, in all four backends, asks every covered member to
  fit, fixed ones included (a `[since]` member absent at this version is
  not in the span), and runs `_region_fits` over the regions the tag
  covers (`traverse.covered_regions`), so a span over a coded or sealed
  region that does not fit the frame is refused, as `validate` refuses
  the message.
- The walker answers `name.covered at+len` (or `refused`) beside `.sum`,
  so the range is compared with every backend's `COVERED_RANGE` and not
  only among the four. A member of a coded or sealed region stands for
  the region (the image's region rows carry its codec now, as
  `region_codec`): `edges.stuffed_seal`'s one plaintext byte is
  forty-eight bytes of stuffing on the wire. The range is rounded out to
  whole bytes; the sum keeps the arithmetic the C walker shares.

## And a nested struct

`edges.blocked_arg`: a tag covering `one`, a nested struct handed an
argument. The four measured `one` inside a sub-view already clamped to
the frame and answered `1+48` where the walker, measuring it unclamped,
refused; `validate` refused through the child's own check. `_covered`
now asks such a member -- one nested struct of no fixed size, not a run
and not located -- through its own accessor, which refuses a struct the
frame does not hold, in all four.

## Measured

Before: the walker sweep failed on dtls, packet and edges (walker
`refused`; C `21+21`, `4+58`, `1+48`, and `covered_tail` `49+0`). After:
60 schemas agree with C on every `.covered` line;
the four-way and the walkers' sum sweep are unchanged.
