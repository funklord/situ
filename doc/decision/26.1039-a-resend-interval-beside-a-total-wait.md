# 26.1039: a resend interval beside a total wait

Status: recorded
Date: 2026-11-14
Source: fuzznet (`suggestion/fuzznet.md` 2026-11-14), and the holder's
agreement to the spelling proposed for it

**A relation could state one per-attempt `timeout_ms` and a retry count**,
so an exchange's total was always (retries + 1) x timeout. fuzznet resends
every 600 ms with no piece of the reply, at most three times, and gives up
at 3 s whatever happened -- sends at 0, 600, 1200, 1800 and a final wait to
3000. 600 x 4 gave up at 2400; 750 x 4 reached 3000 but resent at 750.

**`[resend_ms = 600, retries = 3, timeout_ms = 3000]` now says it.** With
`resend_ms`, `timeout_ms` is the whole exchange's wait: a slot keeps that
end beside its next deadline, which is the sooner (serially, as 26.1008's)
of the next resend and the end, or the end once no resend is left; a due
step expires the exchange when none is left or the end has passed, and
otherwise resends. `wellformed` refuses an interval no shorter than the
total, which resends nothing, and one with no policy to divide.

**Without `resend_ms` nothing changes**: every driver generates exactly
what it did, so the mode costs nobody who does not state it. With it, each
driver's `init` (C), constructor (C++, Rust) or `__init__` (Python) takes
the interval after `retries` as a deployment override, as the other two
are, and a `RESEND_MS` constant names the schema's.

**Measured** in all four drivers, step by step: nothing sent at 599; a
resend at 600, 1200 and 1800, each waking next at the following one and
then at 3000; nothing at 2999; the exchange expired at exactly 3000 with
nothing left to wait for. Rust built with overflow checks on.
