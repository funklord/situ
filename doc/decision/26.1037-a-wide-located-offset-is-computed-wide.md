# 26.1037: a wide located offset is computed wide

Status: recorded
Date: 2026-11-14
Source: openmlx4's MFA reproduction (inbox 20261114T0025Z), compiled in C
while following it up

**C and C++ computed a located offset in 32 bits.** openmlx4 places its
MFA data at `(toc.data_offset_high << 32) | toc.data_offset_low`. Shifting
a 16-bit field by 32 is undefined behaviour in both languages -- gcc's
UndefinedBehaviorSanitizer: "shift exponent 32 is too large for 32-bit type
'int'" -- and the result was cut to 32 bits, so with the high half 1 the
accessor served the two bytes at offset 0x18 for an offset of
0x1_0000_0018 and answered OK. Python's integers and Rust's `usize` had it
right.

**Both now compute it as a size expression's leaves are** -- each held to
`SITU_LEAF_MAX` and the sum in 64 bits -- refuse it past the message, and
only then narrow it. Measured: the high-half vector is BOUNDS and the low
one serves `ab cd` from 0x18, in C (under UBSan) and in C++; the C check
fails against the previous emitter, with the sanitizer's report.

**And a change taken back, which is the half worth reading.** Following
the same reproduction, all four readers' `validate` and both walkers were
first made to refuse a located member the message does not reach. That
reversed a settled decision: 26.446 and section 9.8 hold that a located
member "reaches past the frame by construction", that its accessor asks
the message on every call, and that a BMP truncated below its pixels is
read rather than refused -- a schema wanting the strict answer says so with
`require`. `test_located_reaches_past_the_frame` pins exactly that, and the
change was withdrawn before it was committed. What it leaves open is
26.1034: `situc verify` now reads every located member through its
accessor and refuses a vector whose member falls outside it, which is
stricter than `validate` by design. Whether verify should follow 26.446 or
keep openmlx4's stricter reading is put to the holder.
