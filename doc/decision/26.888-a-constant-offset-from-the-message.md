# 26.888: A constant offset from the message

Status: recorded
Date: 2026-11-06
Source: the holder, answering the question 26.667 left

26.667 asked whether the offset axis should distinguish a message-relative
constant, and the holder said yes.

    u32 signature at 1;      offset=MessageStatic(0x01)  align=Aligned(1)
    u32 board_id  at 26;     offset=MessageStatic(0x1A)  align=Aligned(2)
    u8  count     at BASE + 42;  offset=MessageStatic(0x8E)

`at` over literals and constants folds to `Placement.located_bytes`, and a
new row, `message-static`, gives the value. `DataPlaced` stays for an `at`
a field decides. What 26.667 said a literal does not earn goes: there is no
`Unstable`, since no field holds the offset, and the alignment is the
constant's. The per-call bounds check stays, because a view still cannot
resolve the number. No generated code changes but one comment.

## Its place in the lattice

    AbsoluteStatic > FrameStatic > MessageStatic > Dynamic > DataPlaced > Scanned

**Below `FrameStatic`**, which a reader resolves from the frame it holds.
This one takes the message, and every read checks the bound. **Above
`Dynamic`**, because nothing has to be read to reach it. Weaker is what the
axis says about reaching a member, and the order is exactly that.

## A fault the value showed

The row for "a dynamically sized member earlier in the same frame" fired on
every located member, since it asks only whether `offset_bits` is None. It
was invisible while `DataPlaced` was weaker and won the meet, though `situc
explain` blamed the wrong member. A located member owes nothing to what
precedes it, so that row and its codec sibling now skip it.

## The image

The image stores an index into the axis, so the value moves `Dynamic` and
everything after it. `FORMAT_VERSION` is 6 in the packer, the walker and
`std/image.situ`, so an old walker refuses a new image rather than reading
`Dynamic` as `DataPlaced`. Appending the value out of order would have kept
the indices, and `capability.py` holds the axis to its documented order for
good reason: the lattice wins (section 11).

## Radiotap is not this

26.667's question was attached to radiotap in the last run's report, as
needing message-relative alignment. Measured, it does not. Radiotap aligns
from its own header, not from the message, and the two agree only when the
header is at byte 0. A pad in the holder's coordinates is right, reads today
through a `pad_to` arm, and is 26.889's.
