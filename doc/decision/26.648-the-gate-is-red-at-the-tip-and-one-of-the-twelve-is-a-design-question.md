#### The walker and the backends disagree about what a count IS

The real finding, and it is not the clamp.

    ble.situ   ('le_advertising_report', 'reports')
       walker: reports count=2        C: reports count=32
    edges.situ ('segments', 'parts')
       walker: parts count=0          C: parts count=234

With the ask on, a struct run's count is compared against the walker
for the first time. The walker answers **how many elements fit in the
extent**; C answers **what the driving field says**, clamped at the
leaf. Both are defensible and they are different questions.

**And the chain that produced it is the lesson.** 26.641 held the probe
back on two obstacles. The first was that the walker had no count for
five such runs, and 26.642 closed it by taking the count from the
extent -- a choice made with care and with its own measurement of what
`size_code` means. It could not be checked against the backends,
because the second obstacle was that the backends disagreed with *each
other*. So the first obstacle was closed by a decision the second
obstacle made untestable, and fixing the second is what finally asked
the question.

That is worth more than the defect: **two blockers on one probe were
not independent.** Clearing one of them under the other's shadow
produced an answer nobody could check, and it looked finished.

Which answer is right is a question for the holder, and 26.644's rule
says to look for it elsewhere in the project first: 0032's closing
invariant -- *no rung invents a fact the schema did not state* --
argues for the declared count, since "how many fit" is derived and the
element accessors bound each read anyway. That is an argument, not the
decision, and `sized_run_count` would be the thing to change.
