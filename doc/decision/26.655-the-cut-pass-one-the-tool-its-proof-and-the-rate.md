# 26.655: The cut, pass one: the tool, its proof, and the rate

Status: recorded
Date: 2026-10-16
Source: `project.md` section 26, where it was written

26.608 measured the problem and left the pass authorised and undone. This
is the first one, and `tool/cut.py` is what performs it.

#### The measurement that shaped the method

716 entries, 43,002 lines, mean 60. **There is no small set of giants to
cut**: the largest 25 entries hold 10 per cent of section 26 and the
largest 200 hold 44, so the weight is spread and this is 716 entries of
reading, about 23 passes at pass one's size.

And the obvious mechanical key does not work. Keying on *"the paragraph
contains bold"* would drop 7,524 of 36,000 entry lines -- 21 per cent --
because almost every paragraph carries some emphasis. The rule is the
bold SENTENCE and the prose around it is the narration, which no regex
separates. So the cut is read rather than computed, and the tool's job
is the proof rather than the choosing.

#### Deletion only, which is what makes the proof mechanical

`CLAUDE.md` requires a mechanical change to state what must not change,
check it, and refuse to write when the check fails. So nothing is
rewritten and nothing is added, by whole paragraphs:

- **every heading survives, spelled identically and in order** -- an
  entry is cited by number from elsewhere, and a lost heading is a
  dangling reference somebody else holds;
- **every `N.NNN` reference that resolved still resolves**;
- **every retained line is a line of the original, in order**, proved as
  a subsequence rather than asserted.

A compressed paraphrase would read better and could not be checked. What
recovers a dropped paragraph is `git log -S` on its own words, which is
the property `evidence.md`'s cut relied on and the reason each pass is
one commit.

**All three were sabotaged and seen to fail**, which is the half a proof
needs most:

    heading removed    1 heading(s) changed or lost: #### The guard ...
    entry 26.35 gone   heading lost, and: 1 reference(s) now name no
                       heading: 26.35
    line rewritten     a retained line is not an original line, in order
    the pass applied   clean

#### Pass one: the fourteen phase entries, 388 lines to 223

26.0 through 26.13 are the implementation plan itself. For a COMPLETED
phase the deliverables list and the `**Acceptance:**` criteria are spent
plan -- neither a rule nor a number -- while the status, the caveats
carried forward and the rules are live. So 26.4 keeps its big-endian
compile-only caveat and 26.3 keeps *a predicate that quietly succeeds
because nothing implements it yet is worse than one that fails*, and the
"Implement X, Y, Z" and acceptance paragraphs went.

42 per cent off those entries, 849 headings intact, the docs gate green.

#### Two faults in the tool, one of which cost four lines

**The entry boundary was wrong.** A body ended at the next heading of
ANY level, so a `####` subheading terminated it: 26.654 listed four
paragraphs of nineteen lines, and the prose under its subheadings was
unaddressable -- which is where most of the narration in recent entries
sits. Bodies end at the next NUMBERED entry now. That also makes the
heading invariant reachable, where before no `--drop` could delete a
heading and the check could not be exercised through the tool at all.

**And I deleted four good lines testing the heading control**, by
passing `--drop 26.654:4` against a listing `head -6` had truncated, so
paragraph 4 was prose rather than the subheading I meant. Recovered by
reading `git diff --numstat` first -- 0 insertions, 170 deletions, all
mine, nothing rewritten and no other session's content in it -- then
restoring from HEAD and replaying the drop list, which is one command.

**That the pass is one reproducible command is why the mistake cost
nothing**, and it is the same argument as one-commit-per-pass arriving
from the other side: a cut that accumulates by attrition can be neither
replayed nor recovered.

#### The passes, one line each

Pass one's record was 130 lines about a 165-line cut, so the file GREW
by that commit. That is the weight 26.608 measured, reproduced in the
act of removing it. This entry is the method, written once; a pass is a
line.

	1  26.0-26.13   the phase entries     388 -> 223   -165
	2  26.628 .646 .636 .649  mine        533 -> 399   -134

#### And the structural move, which the holder chose instead

The projection below was put to the holder and the answer was to move
the cited entries to `doc/decision/`. The measurement that decides the
naming: section 26's entries are cited about 4,000 times, **2,652 of
them from source** -- `codegen/c/emit.py` alone cites 109 -- across 380
distinct entries, with 435 more cited from the document. So **a moved
record keeps its own number**, `26.NNN-slug.md`, rather than taking the
next in the `NNNN` sequence: renumbering would invalidate every one of
those sites, in code comments nobody would think to grep.

	move 1  the ten most-cited   48,865 -> 47,111   -1,754
	move 2  125 cited            47,111 -> 38,674   -8,437
	move 3  190 cited            38,674 -> 27,737  -10,937
	move 4  the last 187         27,737 -> 15,227  -12,510
	move 5  the 143 uncited      15,257 ->  8,367   -6,890

**Done. 655 records, nothing in section 26 left inline.** `project.md`
is 8,367 lines and about 104,000 tokens -- half a context window, from
the three 26.608 measured, and the thing the cut could not reach.
Section 26 is 2,710 of those lines, 32 per cent, where it was 88. The 62
original `NNNN` decision records are untouched.

**The uncited 143 moved too, on the holder's instruction 2026-10-17,
and that overrides a test I had cited twice.** `harmonization.md` says
where records are not being cited they have not earned the directory,
which is why they stayed in move 4. Two things make the override the
better answer, and both are measurements rather than preferences: with
143 entries inline the document was 202,000 tokens, which is *over* a
200,000 window and so still failed the thing the whole exercise was
for; and once 512 of 655 are records, the 143 are the inconsistency
rather than the rule -- a reader following one finding opens a file and
following the next scrolls.

So the test is about whether a SECOND document is earned, which these
655 citations settled, and not about which members of one population
live where.

**This entry is itself one of the 512**, which is the first thing the
move taught: an attempt to append this table to `project.md` failed on
its own anchor, because 26.655 is cited and had moved out from under it.
A document that cites itself heavily becomes a directory that cites
itself heavily, and the edit site moves with the record.

**What the move cost that the cut would not have.** Section 26 is no
longer readable as prose: it is 512 headings over one-line pointers, and
following a finding is now a file open. That is the trade a consulted
document makes for being consultable, and the same one `evidence.md`
made when its incidents went to the commits -- except here the text
stays in the tree rather than in the history, which is the better half
of the bargain.

`tool/split.py` carries the proof: every moved line lands in exactly one
record byte-identical, every heading stays in `project.md` over a
one-line pointer, and nothing else in the document changes.

**And the pointer check is the tool's, not the gate's.** `split.py` said
in its own docstring that the `docs` mode proves every pointer resolves,
which is false: `doc_paths` reads table rows only, deliberately, because
a path in prose is as often an example or a glob. The gate reported `4
path(s)` over a 47,000-line document and I read its "names no missing
file" as covering ten pointers it had never looked at. `--check` is the
check that sentence described, and it was seen to fail on a renamed
file.

**Two conflicts with that directory's convention, named rather than
resolved.** `harmonization.md`'s settled inventory spells a record
`NNNN-slug.md`, which `26.NNN-slug.md` deviates from -- forced by the
citations, and the inventory is the holder's to move. And the directory's
rule is that a record is *not edited once accepted*, where section 26's
entries are a living log: 26.648 was struck through and this entry
amended twice on the day they moved. Whether findings in that directory
are exempt from that rule, or the rule now bends, is the holder's.

#### What the cut will and will not reach, measured at pass two

**The yield is a property of the entry KIND, not of the reading.** A
completed phase's deliverables and `**Acceptance:**` criteria are spent
plan with a mechanical signature, and gave 42 per cent. Session-log
entries gave 15 to 20, because they were written as rules. The
signature is now exhausted: one `**Acceptance:**` paragraph, two lines,
remains in the document.

So the projection, which the holder should have before the other 21
passes are spent: the document is 80 to 85 per cent rule by its own
measure, the cut takes 7,000 to 9,000 lines over about 700 entries of
reading, and 49,000 lines becomes roughly 41,000 -- **still three
context windows.** The cut is worth doing and does not make this file
readable in one session.

What would is structural, and is not a cutting pass: splitting section
26, or moving the entries cited by number into `doc/decision/`, which
`harmonization.md` names as exactly the case a second document earns --
303 citations by number across two trees were what justified that
directory. **Whose decision that is, is the holder's**, and naming the
option with its cost is the whole of what this entry does with it.
