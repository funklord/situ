# 26.712: six hundred and forty-three dates

Status: recorded -- repaired, with the proof 26.689 specified, and gated
Date: 2026-10-28
Source: `project.md` section 26

26.689 found that 645 of the records in `doc/decision/` had a diff hunk
header where their `Date:` should be, left it, and named the proof the
repair would have to carry. This is that repair. 643 remained; two were
fixed in passing by 26.710 and 26.711, which were editing those records
anyway and could date them exactly.

## What was wrong

    Date: @ -21417,0 +21418,56 @@ where they parted, twice, in the half-...

The bulk move of 2026-10-17 spliced the hunk header of the diff it was
applying into the field. A record in this directory opens with `Status:`
and `Date:` -- that is the shape every one of them claims to have -- so it
was broken in 643 of 776 places by a tool whose output nobody checked.

**And nothing looked.** `test_conventions.py` gated the `Status:` line
beside it and said nothing about `Date:`, which is why eleven days passed
with 83 per cent of the directory carrying an unreadable shelf-life field.

## The dates, and why one walk rather than 643 queries

26.689 named the per-record method, `git log -S"### 26.260 "` over
`project.md`, and also why not to use it 643 times: about nine seconds
each. So the dates come from **one reverse walk** of `git log -p --
project.md`, taking the first commit that ADDS each `### 26.N` heading.
914 commits, 6.5 MB of patch, one pass, 709 entry numbers dated.

**Corroborated against the method it replaced**, because one instrument is
not evidence: five records queried both ways agree, including 26.260,
whose date 26.689 had computed independently as 2026-09-05. Five more at
random after the write agree too.

## The proof, checked in the tool

26.689 specified it and the tool refuses to write unless it holds for
every file: the rewritten line matches `^Date: \d{4}-\d{2}-\d{2}$`, the
line count does not move, **exactly one line differs**, and no date is
later than the move that lost it. A refusal anywhere stops the whole pass,
because a proof that holds for 640 files and not 3 is not a proof -- which
is the fault being repaired, stated as a rule.

Afterwards, from outside the tool:

    643 files changed, 643 insertions(+), 643 deletions(-)
    every changed line is a `Date:` line                     643 of 643
    every new value matches YYYY-MM-DD                       643 of 643
    records with a readable date                             776 of 776
    records still carrying a hunk header                       0

All 643 were recoverable and none dated after 2026-10-17, so nothing was
guessed and nothing was left.

## Why a wrong date would have been worse than the broken one

This is the reason to recover rather than invent, and it decided the
shape of the whole pass. `Date: @ -21417,0 +21418,56 @@` is **visibly not
a date**: it announces itself, and a reader distrusts the field. A
plausible-but-wrong date does not, and `evidence.md`'s shelf-life rule is
about exactly that -- the licensing entries that went stale were not
wrong when written, they were dated facts recorded without their date, and
what cost was that nobody could see which.

So the tool reports and refuses rather than falling back: no `git log`
sighting, no write. It needed to refuse nothing.

## The guard

`test_every_record_opens_with_a_readable_date` is the cheapest half of
the proof, standing where the next bulk move will meet it. A date and not
merely a non-empty field, because the value's whole job is to say how old
a claim is.

It is the same shape as 26.710's tuple guard and 26.689's own argument: a
mechanical change over many files has to carry a proof, and the 643 are
what it costs when one does not.
