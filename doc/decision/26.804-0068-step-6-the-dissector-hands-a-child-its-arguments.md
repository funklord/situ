The Lua dissector now sizes and dissects `ent` with `vol.block`, and

## How a value reaches the child

Wireshark's `Dissector:call(tvb, pinfo, tree)` takes no arguments, so a
value cannot travel as one. A struct's reads of its parameters go
through a table, `<s>_arg.<name>`, whose `__index` falls back to
`<s>.prefs`. At the top of a capture the child reads its preference, as
0050 has it. A parent wraps each call into the child, both the extent
function and the dissector, in `handing(<s>_arg, {name = value}, f,
...)`. That sets the values for the call and restores what was there
afterwards, through `pcall`, so a call that raises does not leave a
parent's value behind for the next packet. A nested chain of parents
restores in order, because each saves what it replaced.

The values are rendered in the parent with `_over_fields`, over the
parent's own base: `0` in its dissector, `at` in its extent function.
Rule 5 is what guarantees there is always something to render: a
constant, a field of the capture, or a `[stream]` parameter, which is
itself a read of the parent's own `_arg` table.

`table.pack` and `table.unpack` are Lua 5.2, which is the floor
`test_dissector.py` holds the output to.

## Tests

`test_a_dissector_hands_a_child_its_arguments.py` runs `test_dissector`'s
comparison on a schema with an argued nested member, an argued arm and
an argued run. The dissector and the walker agree about every byte both
show, the parent hands its field's value, and the run says it is
declined. Against the dissector before 26.803, the comparison differs
on `after`. `test_dissector.py` holds `edges`' `hands_on` and
`hands_on_arms` to the walker the same way. 26.803's test asserted the
decline, and it goes with the decline.
