# 26.756: `octal` is a radix

Status: recorded -- implemented; half of hull's section 19
Date: 2026-10-30
Source: `project.md` section 26; `suggestion/hull.md` section 19

`radix` was `decimal | hex | scaled`, and the parser's comment said why
two integer bases: "these are the two that appear in the protocols this
targets; a third would need a reason beyond being arithmetically
possible". hull gave the reason: it reads tar from registries and from
`docker export`, and every number in a ustar header is octal text. cpio's
portable `odc` header is the plainer case -- fixed-width octal fields with
no terminator at all.

## What it took

The keyword. Every runtime's digit parser -- `situ_parse_uint`, C++'s
through it, Rust's `parse_uint`, Python's -- and both walkers' read
against a base from 2 to 16 and refuse a digit at or above it, so 8 and 9
are refused in octal without a line of their own; the formatter writes
in any base too. `gen-checks` spelled a field's least value in decimal or
upper-case hex and now in octal as well. The grammar, section 7's copy of
it, section 8.6.2 and the README name the keyword.

`test_an_octal_text_number.py` holds all four and both walkers on an
`odc` header: `000644` is 420, an `8` is refused, and `777777` is the
largest six digits hold; it fails with the keyword removed.

## What it does not do

ustar's numbers are octal digits followed by a NUL or a space inside the
field's width, and a fixed-width text number refuses both -- the padding
rule that makes `007` the only spelling of seven. Where a fixed-width
number's digits may stop short is a language decision, and GNU's
base-256 form is a second one; both are in the open register.
