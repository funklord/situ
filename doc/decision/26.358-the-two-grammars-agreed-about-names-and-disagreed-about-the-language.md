# 26.358: The two grammars agreed about names and disagreed about the language

Status: recorded
Date: @ -28187,0 +28192,48 @@ delimiter, and a COBS decoder finds a frame's end by that byte.
Source: `project.md` section 26, where it was written

**Found while reaching for the grammar to add a production to it.** 0048
had just changed what `ratio_bounded` accepts, and neither
`doc/grammar.ebnf` nor project.md section 7 said so -- the commit that
built it updated the parser and both documents trailed it. That is the
failure `test_grammar_sync.py` was written against, arriving by the one
route its checks cannot see.

**Three witnesses, and none of them reads a right-hand side.** The file
checks that every production section 7 declares appears in the extracted
grammar, and that every enum member reaching the surface is named
somewhere. Both are about NAMES. A production can be in both files and say
different things in each, and five of them did:

    decl        no namespace, tokens, endian_marker or register_block
    member      no marker_field
    field       no `peek` (0057) and no `at` (0042)
    radix       no `scaled` (0056)
    codec_prop  no `kernel =`, which is how a derived codec is declared

Every one in the same direction: **section 7, the authoritative document,
trailing the parser** for constructs that are built and shipped. And each
is a false statement rather than an omission -- `radix = "decimal" |
"hex"` says those are the radixes, and there are three.

**The enum witness cannot cover them, which is why they survived.** It
works by enumerating what the parser turns source text into, so it reaches
a spelling that IS an enum member. `peek`, `at` and `kernel =` are none of
them. Neither is `+ digits` after a ratio, which is how 0048's own change
slipped past two documents in one commit.

**The apparatus was wrong first, twice over.** A first extractor read to
the next production rather than to the closing `;` and did not strip EBNF
comments, so it reported seventeen differences of which ten were prose it
had swallowed. Stripping `(* ... *)` and anchoring on `;` gives seven, of
which two are whitespace and alternative ORDER -- not semantic in EBNF,
and fixed by making the files match rather than by teaching the check to
ignore it. Five are real. A count read off the first extractor would have
been wrong by a factor of two in the direction that makes the finding look
bigger.

**The asymmetry stays deliberate.** `doc/grammar.ebnf` may declare
productions section 7 has not absorbed, and does -- sixteen of them, which
is the arrangement its header describes. What the new check refuses is the
two files declaring one production and disagreeing about it, over the 65
they share.
