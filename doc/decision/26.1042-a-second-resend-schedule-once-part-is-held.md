# 26.1042: a second resend schedule, once part of the reply is held

Status: recorded
Date: 2026-11-17
Source: fuzznet (inbox 2026-11-14, recorded as 4e4a628e), and the holder's
approval of the spelling fuzznet proposed

**fuzznet could not move its resends onto the driver.** It measured a 960 KB
fetch at 20 % loss, replies of up to 67 pieces combined across resends in one
reassembly slot: 600 ms and three resends in a 3 s wait took 24.5-31.5 s,
300 ms and eight took 8.0-8.5 s. A fixed 300 ms everywhere would resend whole
replies on any link slower than that before the first could arrive, so
fuzznet resends at 300 ms, up to 8 times, only once some piece of the reply
has arrived -- then the path works, and what is missing was lost rather than
slow -- and keeps 600 ms and 3 while nothing has: 8.5 s. 26.1039's one
interval could not say that.

**`[resend_part_ms = 300, retries_part = 8]` beside `resend_ms` now says
it.** Only the consumer knows a piece has arrived, since reassembly is its
own, so each driver gains `part_held(id, now)` (`_part_held` in C). From the
first such call the exchange's next resend is due `resend_part_ms` after the
last send -- at once if that has passed -- and `retries_part` bounds every
resend it has had or will have, as fuzznet's one counter does: eight in all,
not eight more. Later calls change nothing. `timeout_ms` still ends the
exchange, and an unknown `id` is CONSTRAINT.

`wellformed` holds the two together, beside `resend_ms` (the schedule they
replace), each positive, and the interval shorter than the total wait.
**Without them nothing changes**: every driver generates exactly what 26.1039
did. With them each driver's `init`, constructor or `__init__` takes both
after `resend_ms` as deployment overrides, and `RESEND_PART_MS` and
`RETRIES_PART` name the schema's.

One part of fuzznet's schedule is not taken: it resends only while
`now + 150 < deadline`, which keeps a resend from landing too close to the
end to be answered. No schema states that margin, and situ supplies no
behaviour the schema did not state, so a resend may still go out 300 ms
before the end, as 26.1039's could.

## Measured

`test/unit/test_a_part_schedule.py`, in all four drivers, step by step: sent
at 0, resent at 600, part held at 700, nothing at 899, resent at 900, 1200,
1500, 1800, 2100, 2400 and 2700 -- eight resends -- each waking next at the
following one and then at 3000, and expired at exactly 3000. `part_held` for
an exchange nobody sent is refused in all four. With the C step resending at
`resend_ms` after part is held, the C case fails. Rust built with overflow
checks on.

The generated Python driver does not pass `mypy --strict`, and did not
before this change either: its `type: ignore` comments name the wrong error
codes on `on_message` and `peek`. Recorded as its own item rather than
folded in here.
