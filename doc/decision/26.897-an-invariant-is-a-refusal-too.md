# 26.897: An invariant is a refusal too

Status: recorded
Date: 2026-11-06
Source: `suggestion/fuzznet.md`, the record store, and the holder's rule that a
sane protocol's need is ours

    struct s { u16 len; rec body; u8 z; }
    invariant s.len == size(s.body);

fuzznet could not tie a length prefix to a nested object's size. The language
could state it, and 16.1 had the construct, but 16.1 describes the writing side
alone: the field loses its setter and a recompute is generated. `validate`
passed a `len` of 9 over a three-byte body, in all four. A schema stating what
no reader holds a sender to is what 14.5 calls worse than silence.

## What changed

**An invariant implies a refusal.** `Schema.whens()` answers with what the
author declared and, for each invariant, `when <field> != <what it derives
from> refuse <struct>_<field>_contradicted`. Everything that reads a `when` has
it from there: four `validate`s, both walkers, the wire signature, the
dissector's expert info. No check kind was added. The unparser reads `decls`,
so it writes what the author wrote.

**`size(member)` renders in an expression.** A `when` over one raised a traceback
out of `over_fields`, an `UnknownName` and not a diagnostic, while `map` accepted
it. `names.with_sums` lifts it as it lifts `sum`, into `Terms.size`, the same
answer the recompute uses. The packer's `SIZE` opcode already served the walkers.

**Rust read an invariant off its `Mut`**, which has no extents, and did not
compile. It reads through the view now.

## Why this was smaller than it looked

It was scoped as a new image check kind, about twenty sites in the C walker
alone. `when ... refuse` already was that check: an expression, evaluated in
`validate`, in all six descriptions. A construct that exists under another name
is the cheapest kind of gap there is.

Only where a reader can evaluate the right side, which is sizes and arithmetic
over them. `offset` and `count` of a member the data places have none, and 16.1
already says what follows: no recompute, the field unwritable, and so nothing
to refuse over. A `when` naming either is refused by `check_when_calls`, where
it had been that traceback.

A stale message refuses too: after a write and before the recompute, `validate`
says what `Message.transmittable` already said.
