# 26.1038: a vector is one whole message

Status: recorded
Date: 2026-11-14
Source: the holder, asking that openmlx4's need be understood and the
tension between 26.1034 and 26.446 settled from it

**The tension.** 26.1034 made `situc verify` read every member placed by
`at` through its accessor and refuse a vector whose member lies outside
it -- openmlx4 had found verify passing one 4 GiB past a 26-byte vector.
26.446 and section 9.8 hold that `validate` does NOT ask that: a located
member "reaches past the frame by construction", and a BMP truncated below
its pixels is read rather than refused.

**What openmlx4 needs, read from its tree** (`tool/schema_check.py`, its
`make schema-check`): `verify` over golden vectors that openmlx4's own
writer produced is, in its words, "the only check here that a wrong schema
cannot satisfy by being consistently wrong about where a field is". Each
vector is a whole FS2 image, VPD block or MFA archive. A member placed
outside it is the schema, or the writer, wrong about where something is --
the exact fault the gate exists for. Its own script already adds what
verify lacked (it ran every declared checksum, with a byte-flipped control,
because verify did not before 26.1023).

**Settled: both stand, and they answer different readers.** `validate`
answers for a reader that may hold part of a message -- the headers of a
file before its body -- and stays as 26.446 left it; a schema wanting the
strict answer there says so with `require`. A vector is by definition one
whole message, and `verify` already held it to more than `validate` does:
no bytes left over (`_surplus`, which `validate` never asks), every stated
expectation, every computable checksum (26.1023). A located member inside
the vector is the same kind of fact, so 26.1034 stays.

**Measured**: every committed corpus still conforms, BMP's located pixels
among them, and openmlx4's reproduction is refused while its in-range twin
conforms (`test_verify`).
